      Why We Celebrate Teacher's Day

      • Dr. Sarvepalli Radhakrishnan was a great scholar, philosopher, and dedicated teacher of India.
      • He was born on September 5, 1888, in Tiruttani, Tamil Nadu.
      • His birthday is celebrated across India every year as Teachers' Day.
      • He served as the first Vice President of independent India from 1952 to 1962.
      • He then became the second President of India, serving from 1962 to 1967.
      • He rose from a very humble background to become a world-renowned academician.
      • He was a brilliant student who earned a master's degree in Philosophy from Madras Christian College.
      • He taught at prestigious institutions like the University of Mysore, University of Calcutta, and Oxford University.
      • He acted as a bridge between Eastern and Western thought, introducing Indian philosophy to the world.
      • In 1954, the Indian government honored him with the Bharat Ratna, India's highest civilian award.
      • When he became President, his students wanted to celebrate his birthday.
      • He requested them instead to observe September 5th as Teachers' Day to honor all educators.
      • The tradition of celebrating Teachers' Day in India officially began in 1962.
      • He firmly believed that "teachers should be the best minds in the country".
      • He maintained that true teachers do not just give information, but help us think for ourselves.
      • He viewed education as a transformative tool to shape human character and society.
      • Beyond politics and teaching, he served as India's ambassador to the Soviet Union.
      • He passed away on April 17, 1975, leaving behind a timeless legacy of wisdom.
      • Teachers' Day reminds us of his deep love, dedication, and respect for the teaching profession.
      • His life continues to inspire both students and teachers to strive for knowledge and humility.
